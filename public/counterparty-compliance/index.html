The certificate is in the folder. That is the whole problem.

Most compliance programs are two people, a shared inbox, and a spreadsheet with a tab for every project. Certificates get collected at signing and never looked at again. Financials get filed, not read. Requests go out, and out, and out.

The people doing it are careful and organized. They were also never trained as risk advisors, so what they can confirm is that a document arrived, not that it protects you.

Four things a certificate will not tell you

The entity does not match

The company that signed your contract is not the named insured on the policy. Close name, different entity. On paper you are covered. In practice you are not.

The policy expired eight months in

A two-year project outlasts a twelve-month policy. If nobody chases the renewal, you are carrying the risk and you will not know until you need it.

The endorsement points at last year

Additional insured and waiver language that no longer attaches to anything current.

The financials were filed, not read

A subcontractor's numbers have been getting worse for three years. Somebody collected them every year. Nobody looked.

Questions about counterparty compliance

What does InFrame do for general contractors?

We monitor every subcontractor on your projects for insurance and contract compliance. We collect and read subcontracts, certificates, and the policies behind them, chase what’s missing, and flag problems before a sub steps on site. Construction insurance experts run the service, so it isn’t another tool your team has to manage.

How is InFrame different from COI tracking software?

COI tracking software confirms a certificate exists and tracks its expiration date. Your team still has to review it, and certificates don’t show exclusions, missing endorsements, or entity mismatches. InFrame reads the contracts and policies behind each certificate, and a licensed expert verifies every flag.

What do you review for each subcontractor?

Subcontract insurance requirements, certificates, the policies and endorsements themselves, named entities, limits, and expiration dates. We also check your own coverage against owner and lender requirements. Common gaps we catch: the entity on the policy doesn’t match the one that signed the subcontract, or coverage lapses mid-project and nobody follows up.

Do our subcontractors have to use InFrame as their broker?

No. Subs keep their own brokers. When we find a gap, we send the sub a clear explanation to forward to their broker. If they want help getting the right coverage, we can place it, but that’s their call.

Do we have to move our insurance program to InFrame?

No. Our compliance service is a flat fee, separate from any insurance placement. If you also choose InFrame as your broker, that compensation is disclosed separately.

How is pricing structured?

Pricing is based on the number of active projects and subcontractors we monitor, not your revenue or number of users, so your whole team can use it. We’ll recommend a tier after a short review of your project and sub volume.

How much work does this add for our team and our subs?

Less than today. We send co-branded requests to subs, collect documents, follow up automatically, and chase renewals before coverage lapses. Your ops team, PMs, and supers get a daily exceptions email, and subs or their brokers upload certificates directly.

How do we get started?

We start with one or two live projects. Share the subcontracts and certificates you already have, and we’ll show you every gap on your own documents.
