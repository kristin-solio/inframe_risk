This year’s renewal is late

Check before you sign

  • Every line on the expiring program appears on the new one, and anything dropped is named out loud.
  • The limits and deductibles are the same, or you know exactly which ones moved and why.
  • Additional insured, waiver of subrogation, and primary and non-contributory wording still match the contracts you have already signed.
  • No new exclusion has been added: residential, action-over, subsidence, prior work, EIFS.
  • Workers’ comp class codes and the rating basis match the work your crews actually do, so the audit is not a surprise bill.
  • Certificates and endorsements can be issued on day one of the new term.

The two-day policy renewal.

  1. 01The quote lands two days before your policy expires.
  2. 02Certificates have to go out Monday, so nothing can wait.
  3. 03You sign whatever is on the table.

Here is why it happens: some brokers do not send the application to market until about 20 days out. Numbers that arrive that late cannot be shopped, cannot be negotiated, and cannot be checked against the contracts you have already signed.

What should be happening, and when.

Six stages. Tap one to see what your broker should be doing, what you need to hand over, and the red flag that says it is going wrong.

120 days Build the exposure base
Your broker should be
Building your exposure base: general liability rated on gross receipts or payroll, workers’ comp on payroll by class code, and current property and auto schedules. On larger accounts, running a pre-loss audit so nothing surfaces for the first time in front of an underwriter.
You hand over
Revenue and payroll projections, your property and auto schedules, and a list of active contracts.
Red flag
Nobody has asked for your numbers by day 100.
90 days Submission goes to market
Your broker should be
Sending your submission to market, built to answer an underwriter’s questions before they are asked, rather than a bare application that invites them.
You hand over
Your sign-off on the submission. Read it. It is how the market sees your business.
Red flag
You cannot get a list of the markets your account went to.
60 days Follow up with every market
Your broker should be
Following up with every viable market, not just the incumbent, and chasing the ones that have gone quiet.
You hand over
Fast answers to underwriter questions. A question sitting in an inbox for a week is a market you lose.
Red flag
No status update since the submission went out.
30 days Quotes in, compared, negotiated
Your broker should be
Collecting final quotes, comparing them line by line against the expiring program, and negotiating.
You hand over
Nothing yet. This one is on your broker.
Red flag
Still no quotes.
15 to 20 days The proposal lands
Your broker should be
Delivering the proposal with enough time left for questions and changes, and walking you through what moved and why.
You hand over
Your attention for an hour. Run the proposal check below before you agree to anything.
Red flag
The proposal arrives inside two weeks of expiration, which leaves no time to question it, change it, or take it anywhere else.
10 days Bind, and get ready for day one
Your broker should be
Binding coverage and getting certificates, additional insured endorsements and waivers ready to issue the morning the new term starts.
You hand over
The go-ahead, and your certificate holder list so nothing has to be rebuilt from scratch.
Red flag
You are still waiting on a binder, or the first certificate request of the new term cannot be filled.

A renewal is not just a price. It is a promise you already made.

Every contract you signed this year has insurance requirements buried in it. When the policy changes at renewal and nobody checks it against those contracts, you are out of compliance on work that is already underway, and you will find out when somebody asks for a certificate you cannot produce.

Additional insured wording

Your contract names a form and an edition date. A new carrier’s standard endorsement may not be the one you promised.

Limits that moved

A contract that requires $2M per occurrence does not care that the quote came in cheaper at $1M.

New exclusions

An action-over or residential exclusion added at renewal can void the exact coverage a contract was written to secure.

Certificates on day one

General contractors pull certificates the week the term turns over. If yours is not ready, crews stop at the gate.
