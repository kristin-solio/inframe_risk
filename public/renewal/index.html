How renewing your policies should work

What should be happening, and when.

Six stages. Tap one to see what your broker should be doing, what you need to hand over, and the red flag that says it is going wrong.

120 days Your broker gathers your numbers
Your broker should be
Pulling together the numbers a carrier prices you on: general liability rated on your gross receipts or payroll, workers’ comp on payroll by trade, and current equipment, property and vehicle lists. On larger accounts, going through your claims history first, so nothing turns up for the first time in front of a carrier.
You hand over
Revenue and payroll projections, your property and auto schedules, and a list of active contracts.
Red flag
Nobody has asked for your numbers by day 100.
90 days Your file goes out to carriers
Your broker should be
Putting your file in front of insurance carriers and collecting bids. A file built so a carrier can price you without coming back with questions, not a bare application that invites them.
You hand over
Your sign-off on what goes out. Read it. It is how a carrier sees your business.
Red flag
You cannot get a list of the carriers your file went to.
60 days Every carrier gets chased
Your broker should be
Following up with every carrier that could write you, not just the one you are with now, and chasing the ones that have gone quiet.
You hand over
Fast answers to carrier questions. One left sitting in an inbox for a week is a bid you lose.
Red flag
No status update since your file went out.
30 days Bids come in and get negotiated
Your broker should be
Collecting the final bids, comparing them line by line against the policy you have now, and negotiating: the coverage first, then the price. The goal is the right cover at a fair number, not the cheapest paper on the table.
You hand over
Nothing yet. This one is on your broker.
Red flag
Still no bids.
15 to 20 days Your options arrive in writing
Your broker should be
Walking you through your options in writing, with enough time left to ask questions and change things, and showing what moved since last year and why.
You hand over
Your attention for an hour. Run the proposal check below before you agree to anything.
Red flag
The proposal arrives inside two weeks of expiration, which leaves no time to question it, change it, or take it anywhere else.
10 days Coverage gets locked in
Your broker should be
Locking in the coverage and getting your certificates, additional insured endorsements and waivers ready to go out the morning the new policy starts.
You hand over
The go-ahead, and your certificate holder list so nothing has to be rebuilt from scratch.
Red flag
Nothing is locked in yet, or the first certificate request of the new policy year cannot be filled.

Your insurance renewal is a promise, not just a price.

Every contract you signed this year has insurance requirements buried in it. When the policy changes at renewal and nobody checks it against those contracts, you are out of compliance on work that is already underway, and you will find out when somebody asks for a certificate you cannot produce.

Additional insured wording

Your contract names a form and an edition date. A new carrier’s standard endorsement may not be the one you promised.

Limits that moved

A contract that requires $2M per occurrence does not care that the quote came in cheaper at $1M.

New exclusions

An action-over or residential exclusion added at renewal can void the exact coverage a contract was written to secure.

Certificates on day one

General contractors pull certificates the week the term turns over. If yours is not ready, crews stop at the gate.
