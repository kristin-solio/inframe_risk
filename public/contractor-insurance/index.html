Questions about working with InFrame

What insurance does a specialty trade contractor need?

Most specialty trade contractors carry general liability, workers’ compensation, commercial auto, and umbrella or excess coverage. Many also need tools and equipment coverage, and some need pollution or professional liability. Your contracts usually set the rest: limits, additional insured status, and the endorsements a GC requires before you start work.

How much does insurance cost for a specialty trade contractor?

It depends on payroll and revenue, the type of work (residential or commercial, new construction or service), claims history, experience mod, limits and deductibles, and how much you sub out. Mid-market programs are priced account by account, so the fastest way to a real number is a review of your current policies and contracts.

Why use a construction insurance broker instead of buying online?

Online quote tools are built for small contractors buying one policy at a time. A mid-market contractor is a different problem. Once you’re running crews, a fleet, and dozens of contracts, the details decide the outcome: which carriers want your trade, how your contracts shift risk onto you, and whether your coverage will respond. A specialist broker handles all of it.

How fast can you turn around certificates of insurance for bids?

Standard certificates go out same day. Our system learns each client’s requirements, so repeat requests for bids and new jobs take minutes. When the GC needs your certs by Thursday, you shouldn’t be waiting on your broker.

Will our coverage meet the GC’s contract requirements?

Send us the contract. We pull out every insurance requirement, compare it against your actual policies (not just your certificate), and tell you what’s missing and how to fix it, ideally before you sign. A licensed advisor reviews every finding.

What is an additional insured endorsement, and why do GCs require it?

An additional insured endorsement extends your liability coverage to another party, usually the GC or owner, for claims arising from your work. GCs require it so your policy responds when your work causes a loss. Contracts often name specific forms, including completed operations coverage that continues after the job ends.

What does “primary and noncontributory” mean?

Primary means your policy pays first for claims tied to your work. Noncontributory means the GC’s or owner’s policy doesn’t have to share the loss. Without a matching endorsement, you can be out of compliance with the contract even when your certificate looks fine.

Companies large enough to run crews, a fleet, and dozens of contracts, and not so large that they are a national broker’s priority account.
