Your broker should understand the business behind the policy.

Insurance decides how you bid, how you staff, and how fast you get paid. Most brokers process the renewal, issue the certificate and repeat, which keeps it a cost you absorb. A broker who understands the work can turn it into something you compete on.

Most trade contractors are getting the first version. That is rarely carelessness. Specialist teams are expensive to run, so brokers point them at their largest accounts.

Background: cutting / demolition, dust, active work

Can I bid the job?

Owners and GCs set coverage requirements before you can quote. If your program does not meet them, you are not eligible, and nobody calls to tell you why you lost.

Can I get on site?

A certificate that takes four days to issue costs you work with a three-day bid window.

Am I covered when it goes wrong?

Completed operations that end at substantial completion. A per-project limit another job already spent. The contract says one thing and the policy says another.

Can I stay on and get paid?

Lapsed subs, missing endorsements and waiver gaps stall pay applications and hold up funds you have already earned.

Questions about working with InFrame

We already have a broker. Why would we switch?

Most people do not switch on price. They switch after a claim gets handled badly, a certificate arrives too late to bid, or a renewal comes back with a number nobody warned them about. If none of that has happened, a review still tells you where you stand. If it has, you already know why you are here.

Do we have to wait until renewal?

No. The review works at any point in the year, and knowing where your gaps are six months out is more useful than finding out three weeks before renewal.

How is this different from a large national broker?

Specialist attention at your size. The largest brokers have deep construction expertise and they point it at their largest accounts, which is a rational way to run their business. We built ours around companies in the $5M to $50M range and we do not do anything else.

Are you selling us software?

No. We are a licensed brokerage and you are hiring people. Technology sits behind what we do, collecting documents, tracking expirations and flagging changes, and that is where it belongs. You should notice the service, not the system.

What size company do you work with?

Commercial trade contractors from roughly $5M to $50M in annual revenue. Below that, a specialist program is usually more than the business needs.

What happens on the first call?

We ask how you actually run: trade mix, contract requirements, fleet, payroll, project schedule. Then we tell you what we would look at first. It takes about thirty minutes.
