09FAQBrightscout module: Questions accordion

Questions about working with InFrame

We already have a broker. Why would we switch?

Most people do not switch on price. They switch after a claim gets handled badly, a certificate arrives too late to bid, or a renewal comes back with a number nobody warned them about. If none of that has happened, a review still tells you where you stand. If it has, you already know why you are here.

Do we have to wait until renewal?

No. The review works at any point in the year, and knowing where your gaps are six months out is more useful than finding out three weeks before renewal.

How is this different from a large national broker?

Specialist attention at your size. The largest brokers have deep construction expertise and they point it at their largest accounts, which is a rational way to run their business. We built ours around companies in the $5M to $50M range and we do not do anything else.

Are you selling us software?

No. We are a licensed brokerage and you are hiring people. Technology sits behind what we do, collecting documents, tracking expirations and flagging changes, and that is where it belongs. You should notice the service, not the system.

What size company do you work with?

Commercial trade contractors from roughly $5M to $50M in annual revenue. Below that, a specialist program is usually more than the business needs.

What happens on the first call?

We ask how you actually run: trade mix, contract requirements, fleet, payroll, project schedule. Then we tell you what we would look at first. It takes about thirty minutes.

Note

"Are you selling us software?" is the most important question on this page and it is here on purpose. From the call: prospects "want to set it and forget it, they don't want impressive AI bells and whistles" and are "almost worried that we're coming in with all this technology… it's too much, it's all exploding." The answer neutralizes it without ever saying AI, which satisfies the guardrail from both directions.

"How is this different from a large national broker?" is written to be generous about the incumbent. Attacking Gallagher by name would violate the guardrail and would also read as insecure to a buyer who probably respects them.

Mark these up as FAQPage schema. This page will pick up organic long-tail traffic on the same questions.

03What gets missedBrightscout module: Question tiles / checklist

Four things a certificate will not tell you

The entity does not match

The company that signed your contract is not the named insured on the policy. Close name, different entity. On paper you are covered. In practice you are not.

The policy expired eight months in

A two-year project outlasts a twelve-month policy. If nobody chases the renewal, you are carrying the risk and you will not know until you need it.

The endorsement points at last year

Additional insured and waiver language that no longer attaches to anything current.

The financials were filed, not read

A subcontractor's numbers have been getting worse for three years. Somebody collected them every year. Nobody looked.

Note

All four are findings InFrame actually surfaced in a free sample review, in Daniel's words: "We looked at tiny things that they would never find themselves. Like the person that signed the agreement with them from the subcontractor is different than the person on the insurance policy for that subcontractor. Like the entity name is different… it's not in the contract. It's just completely different entity."

These are specific enough to be disprovable, which is what makes them credible. Generic "gaps in coverage" language would land as marketing. "The signer is not the named insured" lands as someone who has done this.

Guardrail check: informed confidence, not dramatized consequence. Each card states the mechanism and stops. The card that could have carried the seven-figure bankruptcy story does not. See module 07.

09FAQBrightscout module: Questions accordion

Questions about counterparty compliance

We already use COI tracking software. How is this different?

Tracking software confirms a certificate exists and the dates are current. That is useful and it is not the same as knowing you are covered. We read the policies behind the certificates and tell you where they do not match what your contracts require. Most clients keep their software. We are the judgment layer on top of it.

Do we have to move our insurance to you?

No. We handle counterparty risk management as its own engagement, whether or not we place your insurance. Plenty of clients start here and never move their program.

Who actually does the reviewing?

Licensed risk specialists. Documents get collected and organized systematically, which should be automatic, but every flag that reaches you has been checked by a person who can explain it.

We manage tenants, not subcontractors. Does this apply?

Yes, and the mechanics are close to identical. Lease requirements instead of subcontract requirements, certificates from tenants instead of subs. We also review the lease language itself, which is where we most often find exposure.

How long does onboarding take?

You can start with one project in a few days. A full program depends on how many counterparties you carry and what condition the documents are in.

What happens to the documents we send you?

They stay confidential and are used only for your review. We will sign your NDA if you have one.

Note

Question two is the most valuable on the page and it is the answer most likely to get edited into something weaker. Daniel was unambiguous: "I charge the GC money to do the risk management for their subs regardless of the insurance." Removing the fear of a bait-and-switch on their whole insurance program is what makes a compliance buyer willing to talk.

Question one concedes that their existing software is useful. Attacking it would insult a purchase the reader personally made and defended internally. Positioning as the layer above it is both more accurate and easier to say yes to.
